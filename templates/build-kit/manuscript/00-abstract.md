# Abstract {#abstract}

## Background {#abstract-background}

Whether statins prevent venous leg ulcer in chronic venous disease is uncertain, because evidence comes from small studies with short follow-up.

## Methods {#abstract-methods}

In this prospective cohort, adults with chronic venous disease (Clinical, Etiological, Anatomical, and Pathophysiological [CEAP] class C2 to C4) from one outpatient clinic were followed for 5 years. Statin use at baseline was the exposure and incident venous leg ulcer the primary outcome. Hazard ratios (HRs) were estimated with Cox regression adjusted for prespecified confounders.

## Results {#abstract-results}

Of 1500 participants, 620 used statins. A venous leg ulcer occurred in 41 statin users and 88 non-users (adjusted HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.55 to 0.94).

## Conclusions {#abstract-conclusions}

Statin use was associated with a lower incidence of venous leg ulcer. Randomized trials are needed before statins are recommended for this purpose.
