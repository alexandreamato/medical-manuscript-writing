# Cover Letter for Initial Submission

The cover letter is read by the editor who decides whether the manuscript goes to peer review. It is a one-page argument for fit, plus the statements the journal requires. The letter for a revised manuscript is different and is covered in `references/responding-to-reviewers.md` ("Cover Letter at Resubmission").

## 1. What the Editor Wants to Know

In the first paragraph, and in this order:

1. **What the manuscript is**: title, article type, design.
2. **The question and the main finding**, in one or two sentences with the key estimate. The editor should not need to open the abstract to know what you found.
3. **Why it matters**: the clinical decision or evidence gap it addresses, in one sentence.
4. **Why this journal**: its readers, its scope, a recent related article or series it published. Name the connection; generic praise ("your prestigious journal") adds nothing.

## 2. Statements Most Journals Require

Check the journal's instructions; many ask for specific wording or a form instead of the letter. Typical items:

1. **Originality and no duplicate submission**: the work has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere.
2. **Preprint status**: whether the manuscript is posted on a preprint server, with the link (ICMJE asks authors to inform the journal).
3. **Prior presentation**: congress abstracts or posters based on the same data.
4. **Related publications**: other papers from the same cohort, trial, or database, and how this one differs.
5. **Authorship**: all authors meet the authorship criteria and approved the submitted version.
6. **Conflicts of interest and funding**: a one-line summary; details go in the forms and declarations.
7. **Ethics approval and consent**: committee and approval number; for Brazilian studies, the approval number from the Parecer Consubstanciado (the Jornal Vascular Brasileiro asks for it, not only the CAAE).
8. **Registration**: trial registry and number; review registration (PROSPERO) for systematic reviews.
9. **Reporting guideline**: which checklist was followed and that it is uploaded.
10. **Suggested and opposed reviewers**, when the system asks: independent experts without recent collaboration; opposed reviewers with a brief, factual reason.
11. **Data availability** and **AI use**, when the journal wants them stated in the letter.

## 3. What Not to Do

1. Do not repeat the abstract or exceed one page.
2. Do not overstate: the letter must not claim more than the manuscript does ("the first study", "definitive evidence").
3. Do not address the wrong journal or editor; reusing a letter from a rejected submission is the most common error.
4. Do not hide overlapping publications or a preprint; editors find them.
5. Do not argue with anticipated reviewers or criticize competing groups.

## 4. Template (Fictional)

```
[Date]

Dr [Name], Editor-in-Chief
[Journal]

Dear Dr [Name],

We submit the manuscript "Statin use and incident venous leg ulcer in adults with
chronic venous disease: a prospective cohort study" for consideration as an Original
Article in [Journal].

In a cohort of 1500 adults with chronic venous disease followed for 5 years, statin use
at baseline was associated with a lower incidence of venous leg ulcer (adjusted hazard
ratio 0.72, 95% confidence interval 0.55 to 0.94). Current guidance on ulcer prevention
does not address statins because prior evidence came from small studies with short
follow-up. Your readers, who manage chronic venous disease in outpatient practice, are
the audience that can act on this finding, and it extends the discussion opened by
[recent related article in the journal].

The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. It has
not been posted as a preprint. Preliminary results were presented as an abstract at
[meeting, year]. All authors meet the ICMJE authorship criteria and approved the
submitted version. The study was approved by [committee] (approval [number]) and all
participants gave written informed consent. The authors declare no conflicts of
interest; the study received no specific funding. The manuscript follows the STROBE
statement, and the completed checklist is uploaded.

Sincerely,

[Name], on behalf of all authors
[Affiliation, e-mail, ORCID]
```

## 5. Brazilian Journals (Portuguese)

1. Write the letter in the manuscript's language unless the journal says otherwise; many Brazilian journals accept Portuguese, English, and Spanish.
2. Formal register: "Prezado(a) Editor(a)", "Encaminhamos o manuscrito intitulado...", "Atenciosamente".
3. State the ethics approval as the journal asks: CEP, CAAE, and the number of the Parecer Consubstanciado; case reports in Brazil also follow CONEP guidance (the Jornal Vascular Brasileiro cites Carta Circular 166/2018).
4. Some journals (J Vasc Bras among them) ask only for specific statements in the letter, such as ethics approval, and collect the rest through forms and the title page. Do not duplicate what the system already collects.
5. Registration of Brazilian trials is in ReBEC (https://ensaiosclinicos.gov.br).

## 6. Checklist

1. Journal, editor, and article type are correct for this submission.
2. First paragraph: what it is, what was found (with the estimate), why it matters, why this journal.
3. Required statements present, in the journal's wording when given.
4. Preprint, prior presentation, and related publications disclosed.
5. One page; no claim stronger than the manuscript.
