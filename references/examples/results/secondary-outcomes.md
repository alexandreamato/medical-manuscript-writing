# Results: Secondary Outcomes

`Use after the primary outcome. Report every prespecified secondary outcome in the prespecified order, with effect sizes and CIs, including the null ones.`

All worked examples in this folder use one fictional randomized trial: 600 adults with venous leg ulcer randomized to supervised exercise plus compression (n = 300) or compression alone (n = 300); primary outcome complete healing at 24 weeks.

## Skeleton

```
% Each prespecified secondary outcome, in protocol order
[SECONDARY 1] [direction and size, with CI] ([Table / Figure]).
[SECONDARY 2] did not differ between groups ([estimate, 95% CI]).

% Multiplicity statement when the protocol says so
Secondary outcomes were not adjusted for multiple comparisons and are interpreted
as exploratory.
```

## Worked example (illustrative; fictional)

```
Median time to healing was 14 weeks in the exercise group and 17 weeks in the
compression-only group (hazard ratio 1.28, 95% CI 1.03 to 1.59; Figure 2). Pain at 24
weeks was lower with exercise (mean difference -6.2 mm on a 100-mm visual analogue
scale, 95% CI -10.4 to -2.0). Among patients whose ulcer healed, recurrence at 12
months was similar (22 of 186 [11.8%] vs 21 of 153 [13.7%]; risk difference -1.9
percentage points, 95% CI -9.1 to 5.3). Secondary outcomes were not adjusted for
multiple comparisons.
```

## Notes and pitfalls

1. Leaving out null secondary outcomes is selective reporting; the protocol and registry show what was planned.
2. Say "did not differ" or "was similar" with the estimate and CI, not "had no effect".
3. Keep the order of the protocol, not the order of significance.
