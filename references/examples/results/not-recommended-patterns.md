# Results: Patterns to Avoid

`Anti-patterns reviewers flag in Results sections, with a better alternative. Examples are fictional.`

1. **P values without effect sizes.**
   - Avoid: `Healing was significantly higher with exercise (p = 0.006).`
   - Better: `Healing occurred in 62.0% vs 51.0% (absolute difference 11.0 percentage points, 95% CI 3.1 to 18.9).`
2. **Interpretation in the Results.**
   - Avoid: `Exercise markedly improved healing, confirming its role in ulcer care.`
   - Better: report the numbers; keep "confirming its role" for the Discussion, proportionate to the design.
3. **"Trend towards significance".**
   - Avoid: `There was a trend towards lower recurrence (p = 0.08).`
   - Better: `Recurrence was 11.8% vs 13.7% (risk difference -1.9 percentage points, 95% CI -9.1 to 5.3).`
4. **Repeating the table in prose.** Give the main numbers and point to the table; do not list every cell.
5. **Denominators that change without explanation.** Every n must follow from the participant flow.
6. **Percentages without counts, or counts without denominators.** Write `41 of 300 (13.7%)`.
7. **New methods in the Results.** An analysis first described in the Results looks post hoc; describe it in Methods.
8. **Subgroup claims from separate P values.** Use the interaction test (see `subgroup-analyses.md`).
9. **Numbers that differ from the Abstract.** Check every number in the Abstract against the Results and tables before submission.
