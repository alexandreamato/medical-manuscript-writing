# Results: Harms and Adverse Events

`Use for every study that exposes patients to an intervention. Report counts by group, seriousness, and events leading to discontinuation.`

All worked examples in this folder use one fictional randomized trial: 600 adults with venous leg ulcer randomized to supervised exercise plus compression (n = 300) or compression alone (n = 300); primary outcome complete healing at 24 weeks.

## Skeleton

```
% Overall adverse events by group
Adverse events occurred in [n (%)] and [n (%)] participants; serious adverse events
in [n] and [n] ([Table]).

% Events related to the intervention and discontinuations
[EVENT TYPE] were more frequent with [ARM] ([n] vs [n]); [n] participants stopped
[INTERVENTION] because of adverse events.

% Deaths
[n] deaths occurred ([n] and [n]); none was considered related to [INTERVENTION].
```

## Worked example (illustrative; fictional)

```
Adverse events occurred in 41 patients (13.7%) in the exercise group and 29 (9.7%) in
the compression-only group; serious adverse events occurred in 6 and 5 patients
(Table 3). Falls during supervised sessions occurred in 3 patients in the exercise group,
none with fracture, and 4 patients stopped exercise because of calf pain. Two deaths
occurred, one in each group, both from cardiovascular causes and not considered related
to the intervention.
```

## Notes and pitfalls

1. "No serious adverse events" needs the denominator and the way events were collected (Methods).
2. Report harms with the same care as benefits: numbers per group, not only "well tolerated".
3. Name who judged relatedness and whether they were blinded (Methods).
