# Results: Primary Outcome Paragraph

`Use for the single prespecified primary outcome. Report both groups, an absolute and a relative effect, and their confidence intervals.`

All worked examples in this folder use one fictional randomized trial: 600 adults with venous leg ulcer randomized to supervised exercise plus compression (n = 300) or compression alone (n = 300); primary outcome complete healing at 24 weeks.

## Skeleton

```
% Outcome, time point, and raw numbers per group
[OUTCOME] at [TIME] occurred in [n/N (%)] in [ARM A] and [n/N (%)] in [ARM B].

% Absolute effect with CI (and NNT when useful)
The absolute difference was [X] percentage points (95% CI [L] to [U]).

% Relative effect with CI
The relative risk was [RR] (95% CI [L] to [U]) ([Table 2]).
```

## Worked example (illustrative; fictional)

```
Complete healing at 24 weeks occurred in 186 of 300 patients (62.0%) in the exercise
group and 153 of 300 (51.0%) in the compression-only group, an absolute difference of
11.0 percentage points (95% CI 3.1 to 18.9) and a relative risk of 1.22 (95% CI 1.05
to 1.40) (Table 2). Ten patients would need to receive supervised exercise for one
additional ulcer to heal by 24 weeks (95% CI 6 to 33).
```

## Notes and pitfalls

1. Report the numbers exactly as in the Abstract and Table 2. A single discrepancy makes reviewers doubt every number.
2. An absolute effect is what patients and clinicians need; a relative effect alone exaggerates small absolute differences.
3. A P value, if the journal wants one, goes after the CI, never instead of it.
4. Do not interpret here ("which is clinically important"): that belongs to the Discussion.
