# Results: Subgroup Analyses

`Use when subgroups were analysed. Separate prespecified from exploratory, and report the test for interaction, not separate P values per subgroup.`

All worked examples in this folder use one fictional randomized trial: 600 adults with venous leg ulcer randomized to supervised exercise plus compression (n = 300) or compression alone (n = 300); primary outcome complete healing at 24 weeks.

## Skeleton

```
% Which subgroups, and whether they were prespecified
In the prespecified subgroup analyses ([VARIABLES]), ...

% Effect within each subgroup and the interaction test
the effect was [similar / larger in X] ([estimate, CI] vs [estimate, CI];
P for interaction = [P]) (Figure [n]).

% Exploratory analyses, labelled
In an exploratory analysis not specified in the protocol, ...
```

## Worked example (illustrative; fictional)

```
In prespecified subgroup analyses by baseline ulcer area, age, and diabetes, the
effect of exercise on healing was consistent (Figure 3). For ulcers larger than 10 cm²
the relative risk was 1.35 (95% CI 1.03 to 1.77) and for smaller ulcers 1.12 (95% CI
0.93 to 1.35) (P for interaction = 0.21). In an exploratory analysis not specified in
the protocol, the effect appeared larger in patients who attended at least 80% of
sessions; this analysis compares groups defined after randomization and may be biased.
```

## Notes and pitfalls

1. "Significant in one subgroup and not in the other" is not evidence of a difference; only the interaction test addresses it.
2. Label exploratory and post-randomization subgroups plainly and explain their bias.
3. A forest plot with the overall estimate is the clearest display.
