# Discussion: Comparison with Prior Studies

`Use to place the result in the evidence: agreement, disagreement, and why. Name the studies; compare designs, populations, and estimates.`

Worked examples use the same fictional trial as the Results examples (supervised exercise plus compression vs compression alone in venous leg ulcer; healing 62.0% vs 51.0% at 24 weeks), and one fictional cohort study (statin use and incident venous leg ulcer, adjusted hazard ratio 0.72, 95% CI 0.55 to 0.94) to show observational wording.

## Skeleton

```
% Agreement, with the specific study and its estimate
Our estimate is consistent with [STUDY/REVIEW] [ref], which found [ESTIMATE] in [POPULATION].

% Disagreement, and a specific, checkable explanation
In contrast, [STUDY] [ref] found no benefit; that trial [DIFFERENCE: shorter intervention,
lower adherence, different outcome definition], which may explain the difference.

% What this study adds to the body of evidence
Our trial adds [LONGER FOLLOW-UP / LARGER SAMPLE / DIFFERENT SETTING].
```

## Worked example (illustrative; fictional; citations are placeholders)

```
Our estimate is consistent with a meta-analysis of small trials of exercise in venous
leg ulcer [CITATION NEEDED], which suggested improved healing but with wide confidence
intervals. In contrast, a trial of home-based exercise found no effect on healing
[CITATION NEEDED]; its intervention was unsupervised and adherence was below 50%, whereas
sessions in our trial were supervised and attendance was 78%. Our trial adds a larger
sample, a blinded outcome assessment, and 12-month recurrence data.
```

## Notes and pitfalls

1. Cite specific studies or systematic reviews, not "previous studies have shown".
2. Explanations for disagreement should be features you can point to (dose, population, outcome definition), not speculation.
3. Do not cite only studies that agree with you; reviewers know the literature.
