# Discussion: Implications for Practice and Research

`Use near the end. Say what a clinician or guideline could do with the result, within what the design supports, and the specific research that is still needed.`

Worked examples use the same fictional trial as the Results examples (supervised exercise plus compression vs compression alone in venous leg ulcer; healing 62.0% vs 51.0% at 24 weeks), and one fictional cohort study (statin use and incident venous leg ulcer, adjusted hazard ratio 0.72, 95% CI 0.55 to 0.94) to show observational wording.

## Skeleton

```
% Practice: who, what, with which trade-off
For [POPULATION], [ACTION] may be considered [where RESOURCES], weighing [BENEFIT] against [HARM / COST].

% Research: the specific open question and the design to answer it
Future trials should [SPECIFIC QUESTION] using [DESIGN FEATURE].
```

## Worked example (illustrative; fictional)

```
For patients with venous leg ulcer treated in specialist clinics, supervised exercise can
be offered alongside compression where physiotherapy is available, with the patient
informed of a small risk of falls. Whether a home-based programme with remote supervision
achieves similar healing, and whether the effect persists beyond 12 months, should be
tested in a pragmatic trial in primary care.
```

## Notes and pitfalls

1. Match the verb to the design: randomized evidence can support "can be offered"; observational evidence supports "should be tested in trials".
2. "More research is needed" says nothing. Name the question, the population, and the design.
3. Do not extend implications to populations the study excluded.
