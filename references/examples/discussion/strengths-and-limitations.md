# Discussion: Strengths and Limitations

`Use to state what makes the result credible and what could have biased it. Each limitation names the problem, its likely direction, and its consequence for interpretation.`

Worked examples use the same fictional trial as the Results examples (supervised exercise plus compression vs compression alone in venous leg ulcer; healing 62.0% vs 51.0% at 24 weeks), and one fictional cohort study (statin use and incident venous leg ulcer, adjusted hazard ratio 0.72, 95% CI 0.55 to 0.94) to show observational wording.

## Skeleton

```
% Strengths tied to bias control, briefly
Strengths include [FEATURE] and [FEATURE], which reduce [BIAS].

% Each limitation: what, direction of bias, consequence
First, [LIMITATION]. This may have [biased the estimate toward / away from the null /
limited generalizability to X], so [CONSEQUENCE FOR INTERPRETATION].

% What was done to address it, when something was
[Sensitivity analysis / design feature] suggests that [IMPACT].
```

## Worked example (illustrative; fictional)

```
Strengths include concealed randomization, blinded assessment of healing from
photographs, and follow-up of 97% of patients. The trial has limitations. First,
patients and physiotherapists were aware of allocation, and patients in the exercise
group had more contact with staff, which may have improved compression adherence; this
would bias the estimate away from the null, although adherence measured by self-report
was similar in both groups. Second, patients were recruited from specialist clinics and
most had ulcers smaller than 10 cm², so the result may not apply to large ulcers managed
in primary care.
```

Observational version (fictional cohort):

```
Statin users differed from non-users at baseline, and confounding by healthier lifestyle
may remain despite adjustment; this would bias the association away from the null. An
unmeasured confounder would need to be associated with both statin use and ulcer by a
hazard ratio of at least 2.1 to explain away the observed estimate (E-value).
```

## Notes and pitfalls

1. "Small sample" and "single center" are not limitations until you say what they do to the estimate.
2. Give the direction of bias whenever you can; readers can then judge whether the true effect is likely larger or smaller.
3. Do not list limitations and then ignore them in the conclusion.
