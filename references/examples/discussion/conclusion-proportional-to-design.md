# Conclusion Proportional to the Design

`Use for the final paragraph (or the Conclusions section). One or two sentences that answer the question, with language the design supports, identical in meaning to the Abstract conclusion.`

Worked examples use the same fictional trial as the Results examples (supervised exercise plus compression vs compression alone in venous leg ulcer; healing 62.0% vs 51.0% at 24 weeks), and one fictional cohort study (statin use and incident venous leg ulcer, adjusted hazard ratio 0.72, 95% CI 0.55 to 0.94) to show observational wording.

## Skeleton by design

```
% Randomized trial
In [POPULATION], [INTERVENTION] [increased / reduced] [OUTCOME] compared with [COMPARATOR],
[with / without] [important harm].

% Observational study
In [POPULATION], [EXPOSURE] was associated with [OUTCOME]. [Trials / further studies]
are needed before [PRACTICE CHANGE].

% Diagnostic accuracy study
In [SETTING], [TEST] had [sensitivity / specificity] of [X] for [TARGET CONDITION];
[its role: rule-out, triage, replacement].
```

## Worked examples (illustrative; fictional)

```
Trial: In adults with venous leg ulcer, supervised exercise added to compression
increased healing at 24 weeks, at the cost of more minor adverse events.

Cohort: In adults with chronic venous disease, statin use was associated with a lower
incidence of venous leg ulcer. Randomized trials are needed before statins are
recommended for this purpose.
```

## Notes and pitfalls

1. The conclusion must not contain a finding that is not in the Results, or a claim stronger than the Discussion.
2. Keep it identical in meaning to the Abstract conclusion.
3. No new citations, numbers, or recommendations beyond the Implications paragraph.
