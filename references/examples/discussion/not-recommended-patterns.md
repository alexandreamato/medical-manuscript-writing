# Discussion: Patterns to Avoid

`Anti-patterns reviewers flag in Discussions, with a better alternative. Examples are fictional.`

1. **Opening with background.**
   - Avoid: `Venous leg ulcers are a major public health problem...`
   - Better: open with the principal finding (see `principal-findings-opening.md`).
2. **Repeating the Results with all numbers.** Restate the key estimate once; discuss what it means.
3. **Causal language from observational data.**
   - Avoid: `Statins prevent venous ulcers.`
   - Better: `Statin use was associated with fewer venous ulcers.`
4. **Generic limitations.**
   - Avoid: `This study has limitations, such as the small sample size.`
   - Better: name the limitation, its likely direction of bias, and its consequence (see `strengths-and-limitations.md`).
5. **Cherry-picked comparisons.** Discuss the studies that disagree, not only those that agree.
6. **Speculation presented as fact.** Mechanisms not measured are hypotheses.
7. **"More research is needed."** Say which question, in whom, with which design.
8. **New results in the Discussion.** Any number discussed must already be in the Results.
9. **Conclusion stronger than the design, or different from the Abstract.** Align both with `conclusion-proportional-to-design.md`.
