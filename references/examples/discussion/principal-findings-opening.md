# Discussion: Opening with the Principal Findings

`Use as the first paragraph. Restate the main answer in plain clinical language, with the key estimate, before anything else.`

Worked examples use the same fictional trial as the Results examples (supervised exercise plus compression vs compression alone in venous leg ulcer; healing 62.0% vs 51.0% at 24 weeks), and one fictional cohort study (statin use and incident venous leg ulcer, adjusted hazard ratio 0.72, 95% CI 0.55 to 0.94) to show observational wording.

## Skeleton

```
% The answer to the research question, with the design
In this [DESIGN] of [POPULATION], [INTERVENTION / EXPOSURE] [RESULT, with estimate and CI].

% The most important secondary finding or harm
[Secondary finding or harm that changes how the primary result should be read.]
```

## Worked example (illustrative; fictional)

```
In this randomized trial of adults with venous leg ulcer, adding supervised exercise to
compression increased complete healing at 24 weeks by 11 percentage points (95% CI 3 to
19), about one additional healed ulcer for every ten patients treated. The benefit came
at the cost of more minor adverse events, mainly calf pain and three falls without injury.
```

Observational version (fictional cohort):

```
In this prospective cohort of adults with chronic venous disease, statin use was
associated with a 28% lower hazard of venous leg ulcer over 5 years (adjusted hazard
ratio 0.72, 95% CI 0.55 to 0.94).
```

## Notes and pitfalls

1. Do not open with background or a restatement of the aim; the reader wants the answer.
2. Use causal verbs ("increased") only for randomized comparisons; "was associated with" for observational ones.
3. The numbers must match the Results exactly.
