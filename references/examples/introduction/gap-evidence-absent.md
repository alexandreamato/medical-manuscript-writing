# Gap: No Direct Evidence on a Relevant Clinical Question

`Use when a clinically important question has not been studied directly, and you can show that with a recent, documented literature search.`

## Skeleton

```
% State the clinical decision that depends on the missing evidence
Clinicians managing [POPULATION] must decide whether to [DECISION].

% State what indirect evidence exists and why it does not answer the question
Evidence for [INTERVENTION / EXPOSURE] comes from [INDIRECT SOURCE: other populations,
surrogate outcomes, expert consensus], which cannot establish [SPECIFIC EFFECT] in [POPULATION].

% Document the absence (with the search that supports it)
A search of [DATABASES] to [DATE] identified no [randomized trials / cohort studies]
comparing [A] with [B] in [POPULATION] for [OUTCOME].

% State the consequence of the absence
As a result, [guidelines / practice] rely on [CONSENSUS / EXTRAPOLATION], and [CONSEQUENCE].
```

## Worked example (illustrative; fictional)

```
Clinicians managing pregnant women with superficial vein thrombosis must decide
whether to prescribe anticoagulation and for how long. Evidence for prophylactic-dose
anticoagulation comes from trials in non-pregnant adults, which excluded pregnancy and
cannot inform the balance between thrombotic benefit and obstetric bleeding risk. A
search of MEDLINE, Embase, and trial registries to March 2026 identified no randomized
trials and two small retrospective series in this population. As a result, current
recommendations are graded as expert opinion, and practice varies between no treatment,
six weeks of prophylactic heparin, and treatment until the postpartum period.
```

## Notes and pitfalls

1. "No study has ever…" is a claim of fact. Make it only after a documented search, and report the search (databases, date, terms) in the Methods or a supplement.
2. Name the indirect evidence and why it is insufficient. An absence stated without that context reads as unfamiliarity with the literature.
3. Tie the absence to a real clinical consequence (uncertain decision, guideline graded as opinion, practice variation). A gap that changes no decision is not worth a study.
