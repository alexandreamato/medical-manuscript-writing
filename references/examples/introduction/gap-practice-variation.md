# Gap: Practice Varies Because Evidence Does Not Guide It

`Use when documented variation in clinical practice (between centres, regions, or clinicians) reflects equipoise or missing evidence, not differences in patients.`

## Skeleton

```
% Describe the variation, with a source
[Surveys / registry data / audits] show that [PRACTICE] varies widely: [QUANTIFIED VARIATION].

% Show that the variation is not explained by patient differences
This variation persists after accounting for [CASE MIX / SEVERITY], suggesting that it
reflects clinician preference rather than patient need.

% Link to the evidence gap
[Guidelines] do not specify [PARAMETER] because [REASON: no comparative evidence].

% State the stakes
If one approach is superior, [N patients / a proportion of patients] may be receiving
suboptimal care.
```

## Worked example (illustrative; fictional)

```
A national survey of vascular surgeons found that the timing of intervention after
transient ischaemic attack with symptomatic carotid stenosis ranged from 48 hours to
more than 4 weeks, and a registry analysis showed a threefold difference in median
time to surgery between the highest and lowest quartiles of centres. This variation
persisted after adjustment for age, degree of stenosis, and comorbidity. Guidelines
recommend intervention "as early as possible" without defining a safe lower limit,
because no trial has compared early and delayed strategies directly. If very early
surgery prevents recurrent stroke without increasing perioperative risk, many patients
may be waiting longer than necessary.
```

## Notes and pitfalls

1. Quantify the variation and cite its source. "Practice varies" without numbers is an opinion.
2. Rule out, or at least address, the benign explanation: variation driven by legitimate differences in patients.
3. This gap supports pragmatic designs (cluster trials, registry-based randomized trials, comparative effectiveness cohorts). Make the closing paragraph's design consistent with that.
