# Closing: Aim, Hypothesis, and Why the Design Answers the Gap

`The default closing paragraph for a confirmatory study. Use it unless one of the more specific closing patterns fits better.`

## Skeleton

```
% State the aim as the answer to the gap just described (PICO / PECO in one sentence)
We therefore [conducted / designed] a [DESIGN] to [estimate / compare / assess] the effect of
[INTERVENTION / EXPOSURE] versus [COMPARATOR] on [PRIMARY OUTCOME] at [TIMEPOINT] in [POPULATION].

% State the hypothesis (direction and, for trials, superiority / noninferiority)
We hypothesized that [INTERVENTION] would [DIRECTION] [PRIMARY OUTCOME] compared with [COMPARATOR].

% Show that the design fixes the specific weakness of prior evidence
Unlike [PRIOR EVIDENCE], this [design feature: randomization / blinded outcome adjudication /
adequate power / longer follow-up] addresses [the specific weakness named in the gap paragraph].
```

## Worked example (illustrative; fictional)

```
We therefore conducted a multicentre, double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized trial
to assess whether 12 months of low-dose colchicine, compared with placebo, reduces the
incidence of recurrent major adverse cardiovascular events in adults with chronic
coronary disease and elevated high-sensitivity C-reactive protein. We hypothesized that
colchicine would reduce the primary outcome. Because enrolment was restricted to
patients with biochemical evidence of inflammation, the trial tests the subgroup in
which prior analyses suggested the largest benefit, rather than an unselected
population, and a minimum follow-up of 24 months captures recurrent events that shorter
trials may have missed.
```

## Notes and pitfalls

1. The aim must answer the gap exactly. If the gap was about patients aged 80 or older, the aim cannot be about "adults".
2. Name the primary outcome and its timepoint here; reviewers check that it matches the registry entry and the Methods.
3. Justify the design by the weakness it fixes, not by self-description. Words such as "novel", "innovative", or "first" add nothing a reviewer can verify; the design feature does.
4. Registration numbers, randomization details, and analysis populations belong in the Methods. One design sentence is enough here.
