# Not Recommended: Introduction Patterns Reviewers Penalize

`Patterns that make the gap unverifiable, disconnect the aim from the gap, or replace clinical reasoning with self-promotion. Each is shown with a better alternative.`

## 1. Abstract conceptual closing without a design

```
% Avoid
Our work reframes how we think about [problem]. By integrating [framework A] with
[framework B], we propose a new paradigm for understanding [outcome].

% Better
We hypothesized that [HYPOTHESIS]. To test it, we conducted a [DESIGN] in [POPULATION]
with [PRIMARY OUTCOME] at [TIMEPOINT].
```

Why it fails: the reader cannot tell whether the study is confirmatory or exploratory, and the Discussion ends up making claims the design cannot support.

## 2. Self-description instead of justification

```
% Avoid
Our innovation is a novel approach that, for the first time, overcomes the limitations
of previous methods.

% Better
Unlike previous cohorts, which assessed exposure after follow-up began, we aligned the
start of follow-up with treatment initiation, which removes immortal time bias.
```

Why it fails: "novel", "innovative", "first", and "contribution" are claims a reviewer cannot check. Name the design feature and the weakness it fixes.

## 3. Unverified absence claim

```
% Avoid
No study has ever examined [question].

% Better
A search of MEDLINE and Embase to [DATE] identified no randomized trials of [A] versus
[B] in [POPULATION]; evidence is limited to [INDIRECT SOURCE].
```

Why it fails: one counterexample known to the reviewer discredits the whole Introduction.

## 4. Vague gap

```
% Avoid
However, further research is needed, and previous studies had several limitations.

% Better
However, the two available trials enrolled fewer than 70 patients each and reported
venous patency rather than post-thrombotic syndrome.
```

Why it fails: the reader cannot tell what the new study must do differently.

## 5. Aim that does not match the gap

```
% Avoid
[Gap paragraph: evidence is lacking in adults aged 80 years or older.]
We aimed to evaluate intensive blood pressure control in adults with hypertension.

% Better
We aimed to assess whether intensive blood pressure control reduces cardiovascular
events without increasing falls in adults aged 80 years or older.
```

Why it fails: the study answers a different question from the one the Introduction raised.

## 6. Strawman prior study

```
% Avoid
Smith et al. was small and poorly designed, so its conclusions are unreliable.

% Better
The largest previous trial showed a reduction in symptoms, but with 120 participants
it was not powered for rehospitalization.
```

Why it fails: reviewers include the authors of prior studies. Acknowledge what earlier work established, then name the specific unanswered point.

## Notes

1. Run this list against the final draft; patterns 2 and 5 are the most common in otherwise good manuscripts.
2. Abstract conceptual framings can appear in the Discussion or in conceptual papers, but not as the closing of an Introduction in an empirical study.
