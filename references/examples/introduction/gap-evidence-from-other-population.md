# Gap: Evidence Exists, but Not in This Population or Setting

`Use when trials or cohorts answered the question in other patients (by age, comorbidity, sex, region, or care setting), and there is a concrete reason the result may not transfer. Supporting signals, such as older observational or mechanistic evidence of harm or benefit in the target group, strengthen the case.`

## Skeleton

```
% State the main evidence and whom it excluded
Recent [trials / cohorts] showed [HEADLINE FINDING], but they excluded [POPULATION] because ...

% Give the reason the effect may differ in the target population
[Observational / mechanistic] evidence in [POPULATION] suggests that [EFFECT MODIFIER],
but those studies were limited by [LIMITATION].

% State the transfer question
Whether [FINDING] holds in [POPULATION / SETTING] is therefore unknown.
```

## Worked example (illustrative; fictional)

```
Recent randomized trials have shown that intensive blood pressure control to a systolic
target below 120 mm Hg reduces cardiovascular events compared with standard control,
but these trials largely excluded adults aged 80 years or older and patients with
frailty, leaving the benefit-risk balance in this population uncertain.

An older line of observational evidence in nursing-home residents suggested that
aggressive blood pressure lowering was associated with increased falls and orthostatic
hypotension, but those studies were retrospective, used inconsistent frailty
ascertainment, and could not separate the effect of low blood pressure from the effect
of antihypertensive medication burden.

Whether intensive blood pressure control reduces cardiovascular events without
increasing falls in adults aged 80 years or older with measurable frailty has not been
tested in a prospective randomized trial.
```

## Notes and pitfalls

1. Give a concrete reason the effect could differ (physiology, competing risks, drug interactions, adherence, access to care). "They were not included" alone does not make a gap, because most results do transfer.
2. Acknowledge the limitation of the older evidence; do not over-rely on it.
3. The closing sentence specifies population, intervention, and outcome; the present study itself is introduced in the next paragraph.
